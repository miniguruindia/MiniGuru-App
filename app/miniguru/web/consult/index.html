T-LAB is not a programme you implement. It is a community you grow, alongside children who elect their own teams, manage their own economy and make things that genuinely matter to them.

We have spent 10 years learning how to build this. We will spend a year alongside you, learning how to build yours.

What makes T-LAB different

Five things that set it apart

Children run it

An elected Planning Team manages the project declaration boards and the weekly sharing meetings. A Finance Team runs the Goin economy, which children use to buy raw resources. A Material Team oversees tools and resources. Children hold real authority, and develop real responsibility.

A realistic economy

The Goin is T-LAB's internal currency. Children earn Goins for making and sharing, spend them on materials, and negotiate the value of their finished projects with peers. Economics, lived and not taught.

Every project is theirs

No assigned tasks. No required topics. Every child chooses what to make, plans it, builds it, fails, iterates and presents it. The facilitator supports, but never directs.

Exploration is valued

Children should receive appreciation as much as resources. Peers give it naturally, and a facilitator learns to give it too, seeing the effort inside every attempt, finished or not. The MiniGuru community backs this need.

A MiniGuru community that motivates

A peer's project from an Indian setting, made from what was around them, triggers a child to try. Goins make the effort feel valued. Views are not the purpose.

Our process

Seven stages, shaped to your school

We customise each stage to your budget and interest.

Common ground

We begin by coming to common ground. We prepare a draft for the first conversation, orient the school and its staff to T-LAB, identify the space, and agree a plan to execute it together, set down in an agreement.

The Teaser Workshop

We spend time in your school, meeting teachers, and run a 3 to 5 day introductory, open-ended tinkering workshop with children. At the end, we and the children reflect on the workshop, and this leads to planning and designing a permanent lab for them. Together they freeze the T-LAB system: the election of the teams, the norms and systems that run the lab, the Goin currency and resources, the planning board, and sharing and selling back their projects.

It ignites their dream, and the lab becomes a co-creation. The school ensures resources, space and a schedule, while children collect junk and prepare to run the space.

Designing and building your T-LAB

A layout, material list and session structure tailored to your room and your school's rhythm, designed with your team, not for them. We work with your staff, an architect and others to build the physical lab to your budget. Zones typically include science, electronics, wood, art, a bank, a planning board, a tool wall and a teacher corner.

Elections with every batch

Each new batch elects its own Idea, Bank and Material teams and begins the cycle again. Children declare their candidature, canvass and ask for votes. Only the election commission, which is also children, applies ink on the finger, the most trusted way to identify those who have voted. This is how a T-LAB management team is chosen, fairly and by the children themselves.

A weekly rhythm

Each child visits the lab at least twice a week, for a full block period, in batches of up to 25. We create a dedicated teacher who stays through the day, and batches have their place in the timetable. Three to four batches visit every day, so 200 to 250 children can use one lab each week. We build the timetable with your school.

During this time children plan and work on their projects at their own pace and interest. They follow the systems they decided together, and discuss democratically whenever an issue arises.

Kickstart and hand-hold

We start the lab and work beside your facilitator and the children for some days on every visit. It is an orientation in how to observe, question and trust, not a one-time training. Support is intensive in the first months, then eases to two to four days every second month. The goal: a lab that runs itself.

Building momentum

To keep the energy alive we organise frequent events through the year, where children share their work with parents and interact with them. Families become part of the lab's story, and children see that what they make is valued at home too.

Questions

Common questions

Does it work in a government school?

Yes. T-LABs run in private schools, government schools and NGO programmes. The model adapts to budget and context.

We already have a makerspace, ATL, tinkering shed or STEAM lab. Do we still need a T-LAB?

Yes, it works with all of them. Each of these spaces gives children room and tools. A child-led system is the engine that runs them, and it makes the same space much more productive.

Where do the materials come from?

MiniGuru is the source: a curated set, a suggested list for each project, and AI search for the rest on Amazon.in at the lowest price. Parents review quality and quantity before ordering.

What if a child does not finish a project?

Every attempt carries planning, hunting for materials and problem solving. Peers recognise that effort, and your facilitator learns to value it too.
