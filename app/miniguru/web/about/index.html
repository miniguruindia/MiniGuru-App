Where it began

A room, some shelves and one question

T-LAB started in 2015 in a room in Trilanga, Bhopal. There were some shelves, a collection of salvaged materials and one question: what do children do when you give them time and real things, and do not tell them what to make?

The first children who walked in were cautious. They looked around. They waited. They asked what they were supposed to do. Then, slowly, something shifted. A child picked up a piece of wire. Another started a clay model. A third began building a boat, which sank, which she researched and which she rebuilt.

That shift, from waiting to making, is what T-LAB exists to protect. MiniGuru exists to make it visible.

Our foundation

The Natural Learning Model

T-LAB and MiniGuru are built on the Natural Learning Model, shaped over 20 years of watching children learn. Children learn most powerfully when six conditions are present together, with happiness at the centre: the state of a child completely absorbed in something chosen for reasons of their own. T-LAB creates these conditions in a physical space. MiniGuru extends them into a connected community.

1

Freedom

To explore without a fixed outcome.

2

Real materials

Things that give real feedback.

3

Time

That follows the learner's pace.

4

Community

Other learners at different stages.

5

Meaning

Work that genuinely matters to them.

6

Validation

Their exploration is seen and valued, most of all by peers. Adults have to learn to do the same, so that children can live their childhood and keep exploring.

The platform

What children do on MiniGuru

A lab is powerful, but it is limited by walls and timetables. MiniGuru takes the same belief online, for children aged 8 to 14.

Project sharing and peer learning

Children document what they made, how they made it, what failed and what they learned, and share it with other young makers. Every project is a real story of making, not a polished showcase.

A community that keeps motivation alive

A child watches a peer's project from an Indian setting, made with materials found around them, and feels the trigger to try something of their own. Views are not the purpose.

The Goin economy

A realistic way to feel valued as someone who creates value. Children earn Goins for making, sharing and kind comments, and spend them on materials.

Project planning tools

Before making begins, children sketch the idea, list the materials and estimate the budget. Planning is where ideas become commitments.

Peers who appreciate

Children understand an unfinished attempt better than any adult, because they have just done the same. Their appreciation keeps exploration going.
