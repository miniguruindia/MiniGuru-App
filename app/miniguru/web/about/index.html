Where it began

A room, some shelves and one question

T-LAB started in 2015 in a room in Trilanga, Bhopal. There were some shelves, a collection of salvaged materials and one question: what do children do when you give them time and real things, and do not tell them what to make?

The first children who walked in were cautious. They looked around. They waited. They asked what they were supposed to do. Then, slowly, something shifted. A child picked up a piece of wire. Another started a clay model. A third began building a boat, which sank, which she researched and which she rebuilt.

That shift, from waiting to making, is what T-LAB exists to protect. MiniGuru exists to make it visible.

Our foundation

The Natural Learning Model

T-LAB and MiniGuru are built on the Natural Learning Model, shaped over 20 years of watching children learn. Children learn most powerfully when six conditions are present together: freedom to explore without a fixed outcome, real materials that give real feedback, time that follows the learner's pace, a community of other learners at different stages, work that genuinely matters to them, and validation of what they explore.

At the centre of all five is happiness: the state of a child completely absorbed in something chosen for reasons of their own. T-LAB creates these conditions in a physical space. MiniGuru extends them into a connected community.

The platform

What children do on MiniGuru

A lab is powerful, but it is limited by walls and timetables. MiniGuru takes the same belief online, for children aged 8 to 14.

Project sharing and peer learning

Children document what they made, how they made it, what failed and what they learned, and share it with other young makers. Every project is a real story of making, not a polished showcase.

Project planning tools

Before making begins, children sketch the idea, list the materials and estimate the budget. Planning is where ideas become commitments.

The Goin economy

Children earn Goins for making, sharing and kind comments, and spend them on materials. Budgeting, scarcity, value and exchange become lived practice.

Materials for projects

MiniGuru is the one source for the materials a T-LAB or Tinkering Corner may need. We have curated a comprehensive set, and we keep a suggested list for each project. For anything else, MiniGuru's AI search looks on Amazon.in for a suitable item at the lowest price and leaves out the rest. Parents buy on Amazon.in themselves; MiniGuru never takes payment in the app.

A community that keeps motivation alive

A child watches a peer's project from an Indian setting, made with materials found around them, and feels the trigger to try something of their own. Watching and views are not the purpose. The Goin economy adds a realistic way to feel valued as someone who creates value.

The sixth condition

Validation of what children explore

In school, creative exploration usually earns no marks, because it does not fit the criteria adults have evolved. Imagine a 10-year-old who starts building a drone and drops it halfway. Most of us would call it a failure, or pay it no attention. Yet the child imagined it, gathered materials and struggled to make a motor run by working out what a complete circuit is. In clear terms, the child has moved from where they were in their mind. Adults often cannot see that. On MiniGuru, children work and watch alongside peers who understand it at once and can appreciate it, and T-LAB facilitators learn to do the same. A child needs appreciation and resources. MiniGuru offers both.

Our offerings

Three ways to start

For schools

T-LAB in your school

A self-governing tinkering community where children manage their own space, economy and projects, with a trained facilitator. Setup follows five steps: a visit and first workshop, a design for your room, a launch with a 3 to 4 day tinkering workshop, facilitator training and a first year of support.

For schools and teachers

Tinkering workshops

Immersive workshops of 1 to 15 days where every participant makes a real project from idea to sharing. No kits, no instructions: real materials, real making, real learning.

For parents

Home Tinkering Corner

A personal making space at home, designed around your child's own curiosity: a short call with you and your child, a layout for even a corner or a shelf, and a materials list matched to their interests.

For students and parents

For students

Make it. Film it. Share it.

Pick a project you love, plan it, build it your way and film it with a phone. Other young makers watch and cheer you on. You earn Goins, climb the levels from Sprout to Legend and become a guru to someone else.

Safe and private

Common questions

Where do the materials come from?

MiniGuru is the source. We have curated a comprehensive set of materials a T-LAB or Tinkering Corner may need, and a suggested list for each project. MiniGuru's AI search finds suitable items on Amazon.in at the lowest price for anything else.

How does MiniGuru keep children motivated?

Through community. A child sees a peer's project, made in an Indian setting with materials found around them, and wants to try. Views are not the aim. Goins give a realistic feeling of being valued for creating something.

Is an unfinished project a failure?

No. Planning, gathering materials and solving problems are real learning, even if the project is dropped. MiniGuru and the T-LAB facilitator recognise that effort.

The T-LAB network

Many schools, one culture

Every school that sets up a T-LAB joins a network of schools, facilitators and children building the same culture, each in their own place and at their own pace. Facilitators share observations, children see each other's projects on MiniGuru and schools exchange visits. T-LABs run in private schools, government schools and NGO programmes across Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Bihar.
