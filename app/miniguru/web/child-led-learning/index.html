What is child-led learning?

Child-led learning is learning where children choose what to make, plan how to make it, and take responsibility for the result. Adults guide, children lead.

Child-led learning in simple words

In a traditional classroom the teacher decides the topic, the steps and the answer. In child-led learning, the child makes real choices. A child decides what to build, plans the materials and time, tries, fails, improves and then explains the result to others. The adult asks questions, keeps things safe and helps the child think. This is also called student agency, learner voice or learning by doing.

Teacher-led and child-led, side by side

Teacher-led

The teacher picks the task. There is one right answer. The aim is to get it correct and hand it in.

Child-led

The child picks the project. There are many ways to solve it. The aim is to think, try, improve and explain.

Skills children build

  • Creativity. Turning an idea into something real, in their own way.
  • Critical thinking. Asking why something works, testing and comparing.
  • Problem solving. Finding another way when the first plan fails.
  • Collaboration. Sharing roles and building with others.
  • Empathy and kindness. Helping friends, listening and giving kind feedback.
  • Communication. Showing and explaining what they made.
  • Resilience. Treating a failed attempt as evidence to learn from.
  • Decision making and responsibility. Choosing, budgeting and owning the outcome.

What a child-led project looks like

  1. The child picks a project, such as a simple robot, a lamp, a game or a model.
  2. They plan the steps and choose the materials.
  3. They build, test and fix what does not work.
  4. They present the result, including what went wrong.
  5. They reflect on what they learned and help someone else.

How schools use it

Schools run child-led learning as a tinkering lab, a makerspace, an innovation lab or an after-school club. The T-LAB model, built on the Natural Learning Model, lets students elect their own teams, manage a lab currency and present their work. Common questions

Does child-led learning mean no teacher?

No. The teacher becomes a facilitator and co-worker. The teacher guides, keeps the lab safe and asks the questions that make children think.

Which ages is it for?

MiniGuru is designed for children aged 8 to 14. The ideas of choice, hands-on work and reflection work at many ages.

Is it only about science and technology?

No. Projects include science, technology, engineering, art and maths, and also craft, design and storytelling. Creativity matters in all of them.

Can parents do this at home?

Yes. Let your child choose a project, help with questions rather than answers, and celebrate the attempt as much as the result. See how MiniGuru works for parents.
